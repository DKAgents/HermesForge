# Breadth Oscillator Confirms Trend Before Counter-Trend Entry

## Discovery Summary

EN041 warns that oscillators lie in strong trends — an oversold reading in a strong uptrend may simply reflect momentum, not a reversal opportunity. The McClellan Oscillator (N186) provides a market-breadth dimension that single-instrument oscillators lack: if the broad market's advance/decline differential confirms the primary trend is intact (McClellan still positive), then EN041's rule to 'buy oversold in an uptrend' gains validity because breadth supports the continuation thesis. Secondary trends (C366) are precisely the counter-trend corrections where oscillators will signal oversold — but only when the McClellan Oscillator has not broken down does this signal represent a secondary correction within a primary uptrend rather than a primary trend change.

## Trading Implication

Before acting on an oversold oscillator signal in an uptrend (EN041), confirm the McClellan Oscillator remains above zero or its own signal line to verify broad market breadth still supports the primary trend; if breadth has deteriorated, treat the oversold reading as a potential primary trend reversal warning rather than a secondary correction entry.
