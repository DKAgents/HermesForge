# Use Volatility-Adjusted RSI to Confirm Reversal Patterns

## Discovery Summary

C149's RSI-vs-stochastics volatility comparison suggests that fixed RSI overbought/oversold levels from N165 are not universal. In higher volatility, RSI extremes need to be more extreme to be meaningful, so reversal patterns should be confirmed only when RSI reaches a volatility-adjusted threshold. Stochastics can act as a secondary confirmation when RSI readings are ambiguous across regimes.

## Trading Implication

Before acting on a reversal pattern, adjust RSI overbought/oversold thresholds based on current volatility rather than using fixed levels, and require stochastic agreement for confirmation.
