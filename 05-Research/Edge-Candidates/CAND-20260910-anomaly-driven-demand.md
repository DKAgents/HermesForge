# Edge Candidate: Anomaly-Driven Demand (ADD) Factor — Factor Crowding as a Cross-Sectional Signal

## Source
**Academic Paper** — Posselt, Anders & Kjær, Mads (March 2026), "Anomaly-Driven Demand," SSRN 6342599. Covered by Alpha Architect (May 22, 2026) and the Quant Newsletter.

### Key Finding
The authors studied **209 published anomalies** from the Chen & Zimmermann dataset and measured what happens when millions of investors simultaneously follow the same mechanical rules to rebalance the same portfolios.

**The ADD Measure:** For each stock, ADD is the net count of anomaly portfolios it enters the long leg of, minus those it enters the short leg of. A stock that becomes a constituent in many long legs simultaneously faces coordinated buying pressure from every factor investor rebalancing at month-end.

### Results

| Metric | Value | Significance |
|--------|-------|-------------|
| **High-ADD vs Low-ADD spread** | 4.03%/yr | t-stat 4.03 |
| **Long-short Sharpe ratio** | 0.61 | Statistically robust |
| **Alpha vs Fama-French 5-factor** | Significant | Survives factor controls |
| **Alpha vs momentum** | Significant | Orthogonal dimension |
| **Alpha vs 3 PCAs of anomalies** | Significant | Not captured by factor structure |
| **Micro-cap ADD effect** | Strongest | U-shaped: mega-cap also strong |
| **Mega-cap ADD effect** | Strong | Weakest in mid-cap |
| **Month-start concentration** | Peak effect | Institutional rebalancing window |

### Mechanism
Returns earned by factor strategies are **partly mechanical** — a portion is generated by the collective rebalancing of other factor investors, not purely by the underlying economic logic of the signals. As factor investing grows, the act of measurement changes the phenomenon being measured. Anomalies that were discovered as passive predictors of returns have become **active agents** in generating those returns.

## Signal
**Stocks with high Anomaly-Driven Demand (entering many long-leg anomaly portfolios simultaneously) significantly outperform those with low ADD, with a 4.03%/yr spread and Sharpe of 0.61:**

1. The effect is concentrated at month-start when institutional investors rebalance en masse — there's a predictable calendar pattern.
2. Not captured by standard factor models — ADD is an orthogonal cross-sectional signal.
3. Works best at extremes: mega-cap stocks (institutions' rebalancing targets) and micro-caps (where rebalancing moves price most).

## Hypothesis
**ADD is a testable cross-sectional factor with our 529-stock universe. We can approximate anomaly membership using available data (value, momentum, profitability, volatility, size, investment) and measure the cumulative "crowding pressure" on each stock.**

### Why This Is Different From Existing Approaches
- Our existing strategies (STR-B, STR-I, STR-Q, etc.) are individual entry/exit patterns (divergence, trend, reversal)
- ADD is a **cross-sectional ranking overlay** — it tells you WHICH stocks to prefer among your signal-generated candidates
- It captures the institutional flow dynamics that our current single-stock strategies miss

## Entry Rules
1. At each week's scanner run, compute proxy ADD score for each of the 529 universe stocks:
   - Count number of anomaly long-leg signals triggered: value (P/B < industry median), momentum (12-1m return > median), profitability (ROE > median), low volatility (60d vol < median), recent strength (5d return > median), volume anomaly (volume spike > 1.5x 20d avg)
   - Subtract short-leg anomaly signals (reverse conditions)
2. Rank universe by ADD score
3. Among stocks that meet primary strategy signal criteria (e.g., STR-B MACD divergence), prefer those in top ADD quintile
4. ADD score is a **tiebreaker/weight multiplier** — not a standalone entry signal

## Exit Rules
1. Re-rank ADD weekly — enter/exclude based on ADD score refresh
2. If ADD score drops below median, reduce position by 50%
3. Standard strategy-level exits still apply
