Dreamforce 2026 is September 15-17 in San Francisco, and the rumor mill is already running at full speed. The confirmed theme is "Becoming an Agentic Enterprise" (a continuation of last year's keynote messaging), and the circulating predictions point to three things: agent-to-agent collaboration frameworks (where agents hand work to each other across clouds), an expanded AgentExchange marketplace with more pre-built agents, and deeper cross-cloud intelligence where agents tap into Service Cloud, Sales Cloud, and Marketing Cloud data simultaneously. A Codleo guide from late July even pegs the current Agentforce implementation failure rate at 67% for orgs that skip foundational work, which is a striking number if you think about what it implies: two out of three teams that try to deploy agents are bouncing off the same wall.

But what can be missed is that the wall they're bouncing off isn't the AI. Atlan published a piece in August that said it plainly: "Most Agentforce rollouts stall on data readiness, not the reasoning engine." The reasoning engine is good. The Atlas engine (Agentforce's core inference layer) can parse intent and build multi-step plans. The problem is it's building those plans on top of data that has 30 to 40% quality issues, which is the figure multiple implementation guides cite as typical for mature Salesforce orgs. You buy Agentforce, you connect your data, and the agent starts returning answers that look confident and are wrong. Not because the AI failed, but because it was reading garbage.

Here's what happens. You buy Agentforce. You spin up a service agent. It reads your case history and starts suggesting resolutions. But your case history has 12,000 overlapping records (a realistic number for an org running 8+ years without a cleanup pass), and the agent can't tell that Case A and Case B are the same customer with the same problem. It serves a redundant resolution. The customer gets frustrated. The agent looks dumb. Your team loses trust in the whole thing. This is the "garbage in, garbage out, amplified by AI" pattern that a Clientell AI analysis from March named explicitly, and it's the reason a Reddit thread from July says "Agentforce provides me zero value without internal business transformation of best-in-breed data quality and governance."

This isn't being discussed enough in the Dreamforce hype cycle. We hear about new agent types, new cross-cloud capabilities, new marketplace listings, new keynote announcements. We don't hear about what happens when you deploy those agents on an org where the matching rules fire on insert but nobody runs retroactive cleanup (the standard pattern: rules catch new issues, but the existing 12,000 just sit there). Tools like Cloudingo, DemandTools, and Plauti exist to clean this up, but they're treated as a consulting project you do before Agentforce, not something the platform helps you with.

The one thing I'd say Salesforce needs to announce at Dreamforce 2026: make data readiness a first-class platform feature, not a pre-implementation consulting exercise. Right now, data readiness is a checklist you run through with a partner before you turn on Agentforce. What if it were built into the agent setup flow itself? When you go to deploy an agent, the platform scans your data foundation and says, "Before this agent can work effectively, here are the 3 data clusters, the 2 missing-field patterns, and the 1 stale-data zone it would run into." Not a general Data Cloud health dashboard. A readiness check scoped to the specific agent you're about to deploy, showing the exact records that would make it fail.

That would change the conversation. Instead of "we bought Agentforce and it doesn't work," it would be "we bought Agentforce and it told us exactly what to fix first." It's the difference between a fence at the door (stop bad data from entering) and a sweep of the house (find and fix the bad data that's already there). Agentforce needs both, and right now it only has the fence. BUT the sweep is where the real value is, because the existing data is what the agents are reading.

This is solvable. Salesforce has Data 360 (the unified data layer announced at Dreamforce 2025). They have the matching engine. They have the quality rules. What they don't have is a guided, agent-scoped readiness workflow that connects those pieces and says, "fix this before you deploy." If Dreamforce 2026 delivers that update, the 67% failure rate drops. If it doesn't, we'll see the same headlines next year.
