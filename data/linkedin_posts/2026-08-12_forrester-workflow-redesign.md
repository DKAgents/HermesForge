Dreamforce 2026 is September 15-17 in San Francisco, and the rumor mill is already running. The confirmed theme is "Becoming an Agentic Enterprise," and the circulating predictions point to three things: agent-to-agent collaboration frameworks, an expanded AgentExchange marketplace with more pre-built agents, and deeper cross-cloud intelligence where agents tap into Service Cloud, Sales Cloud, and Marketing Cloud simultaneously. All exciting stuff. But the predictions are about what agents can do, not how teams actually adopt them.

Forrester just published a piece called "The State Of Agentic AI In 2026: Companies Are Chasing, Few Are Catching." Brian Hopkins and his team report that three-quarters of enterprise leaders say they're adopting agentic AI, but only a small minority have it running in meaningful production. One line stuck with me: "Agents bolted onto human-paced legacy workflows produce task savings, not step-change value." That's the gap nobody is talking about at Dreamforce. We hear about agent capabilities. We don't hear about workflow redesign.

Here's what happens. You buy Agentforce. You pick a high-friction process, say, inbound lead qualification. Today a human reads the lead, checks the account history, routes it to the right rep, and writes a summary. You bolt an agent onto that workflow. The agent reads the lead, checks the account, drafts the summary, and the human reviews it before routing. You saved 10 minutes per lead. Task savings. But the workflow is exactly the same. The human is still in the loop doing the same steps, just faster. Forrester's point is that this is what most teams do, and it's why most agentic AI deployments never scale past pilot.

What step-change value looks like: you redesign the workflow so the agent handles lead qualification, routing, and initial outreach autonomously. The human moves from "review every lead" to "manage exceptions and coach the agent on edge cases." That's a different job. The roles change. The approval gates change. The metrics change (you stop measuring leads processed per hour and start measuring conversion rate of agent-handled leads). That's what Forrester means by "redesign the work, not just the tooling." Pick a few high-friction workflows and rebuild the roles and approvals around autonomy.

This is hard. It's harder than buying Agentforce. It requires someone who understands the current workflow deeply enough to know which steps to automate, which to eliminate, and which to restructure. It requires change management because someone's job is about to look different. BUT this is where the actual ROI lives. Forrester says ROI uncertainty is what traps teams in pilot mode. The reason they can't justify production is they're measuring task savings (10 minutes per lead) instead of step-change value (2x conversion rate from autonomous qualification).

So the thing I hope Salesforce addresses at Dreamforce 2026: don't just show us what agents can do. Show us how to redesign the work around them. Give us a framework for identifying which workflows are worth rebuilding, which roles need to change, and what the new metrics look like. Agentforce has pre-built agents. What it doesn't have is a pre-built workflow redesign playbook.
